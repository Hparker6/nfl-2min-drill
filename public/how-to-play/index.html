How to play: call the plays and run the 2-minute drill

The 4th Call is a football strategy game about the calls at the end of a close game. You get the score, the clock, the timeouts and 22 players from real pro rosters. Call every play, manage the clock, and win it. Every call is graded against every option you had.

Controls

Phone and tablet

  • Tap a tab (Run, Short, Deep, RPO, Trick) and tap a play card.
  • On a pass, tap a receiver on the field to throw to him. Tap him again to change his route.
  • Press and hold a player to see his overall rating.
  • Tap Snap to run the play.

Keyboard

  • 1–9 pick a play in the open tab
  • R S D O T switch offense tabs
  • Z M B G switch defense tabs
  • Enter snaps the play (or calls the defense)
  • Hover a player for his full ratings

Calling plays on offense

The playbook has 27 calls in five tabs: Run, Short pass, Deep pass, RPO (run-pass option) and Trick. Each card shows the play drawn on the field, its formation (Shotgun, Gun Trips, I-Form, Pistol…) and how risky it is. Field goals, punts and kneel-downs appear when they matter, and on fourth down they sit right at the top.

  • Runs: pick the strong side (toward the tight end, an extra blocker) or the weak side (fewer blockers, fewer defenders).
  • Passes: choose the receiver and his route on the field, then add a signal: Play action (fake the handoff), Roll out (move the pocket, fewer sacks) or Sideline (get out of bounds after the catch to stop the clock).
  • Read the defense: before each snap you get a pre-snap read of what the defense is showing. It's right about 7 times in 10.

Managing the clock

When the clock is running between plays, you also choose how fast to get to the next snap. The tempo starts on the usual choice, so you can snap in one tap:

No-huddle

Snap fast, about 13 seconds. The default when you're behind or under two minutes.

Huddle

Normal pace, about 27 seconds.

Milk it

Snap as the play clock expires, about 38 seconds. The default with the lead.

Or stop it: Timeout (you have up to three), Spike (stops the clock, costs a down) or Kneel when you lead. Near the end, Run it down lets the clock run to 0:03 and then calls timeout for a last kick.

Calling the defense

When they have the ball you pick from 19 defensive plays in four tabs, Madden style:

Zone

Cover 3, Cover 2, Cover 4 Quarters, Tampa 2, Prevent. Keep everything in front.

Man

Cover 1, 2-Man Under, Press Man, Cover 1 Robber. Tight coverage; rubs and crossers get open.

Blitz

Cover 0, Fire Zone, Mike, Corner and Safety blitzes. Sacks and hurried throws, but someone is left open.

Goal Line

Goal Line 6-5, Goal Line Blitz, Goal Line Man, Bear 46, Run Blitz. Stop the run.

What each defense stops, and what it gives up

The same trade-offs the game uses. Pick the defense that takes away what the offense needs right now: deep shots when they need a touchdown, the sideline when they need to stop the clock, the run when a first down ends it.

Reading their defense

Tells

Before the snap the defense gives itself away: safeties deep, corners pressed, linebackers walking up. The players showing it glow on the field. Your read is right about 7 times in 10.

Their coordinator

Every defense has a habit: blitzing on third down, sitting in zone on long yardage, playing man in the red zone, loading the box on short yardage. The scouting report says which, and the call panel tracks what they've done this game.

Adjustments

Call the same play three times in a row and he adjusts: runs meet a loaded box, quick throws see press man, deep shots a deep shell. Mix it up.

Momentum and the crowd

Big plays, first downs and stops swing momentum, and momentum sharpens your read (or clouds it). On the road, the crowd makes third down harder to read.

How calls are graded

Before the result is shown, the game plays out the rest of the game thousands of times for every option you had, and compares your call's win chance with the best one. A good call that fails still counts as good, and a bad call that works still counts as bad.

  • Great call: the best call, or within 0.75 points of win chance
  • Close: within 2.5 points
  • Costly: within 6 points
  • Big miss: more than 6 points off

Your Performance score (0–100) is 55% Decisions (how much win chance you gave up over the game) and 45% Result (a win scores 75–100, a loss 0–25, higher inside its band the harder the spot). The Daily score goes on the leaderboard.

The game went to overtime and was won there, but three calls on this drive gave away real win chance, and they are the ones people argue about every Sunday:

  • The deep post on 2nd and 10 at 1:09 gained 23 yards and still graded a big miss. A dig over the middle offered about 10 points more win chance: a deep post is incomplete far more often than it connects, and down 3 with a minute left you need a completion on every snap more than you need one big one. The result was good; the call was not.
  • The timeouts at 1:13 and 0:40 each cost about 3 points. With the clock running and more than a minute left, a no-huddle snap keeps the timeout for the end of the drive, when a stopped clock is worth the most.
  • The field goal on 4th and 4 at 0:37, down 3, tied the game and graded a big miss: going for it was worth about 10 points more. A tie only earns a coin flip in overtime, and 4th and 4 from the 17 converts often enough that keeping the drive alive, with the touchdown and the kick both still in play, beats settling for the tie.

That is what the grade measures: the win chance of the call you made against the best call available, not whether the play worked. The pattern across the drive is the same one the two-minute drill section teaches: throw to the middle when you have timeouts or the clock is stopped, save the timeouts for the end, and know what a field goal is worth before you kick it.

Game modes

Daily

One game a day, the same for everyone, new at midnight UTC. The situation rotates: a two-minute drill, a goal-line stand, a fourth-down call, closing out a lead…

Survivor

Random late-game spots back to back. Win to keep going; one loss ends the run.

Practice

Two-minute drill practice without the scoreboard: build any spot (score, clock, field position, timeouts, weather) and play it with Coach mode, which shows every option's win chance first. Repeat a fourth-down call or a goal-line stand until the decision is automatic.

Career (beta)

Create a quarterback and take him from one college game through draft night and a full season in a fictional league. How Career works.

Career mode

Career is a fictional league: 32 made-up teams in two conferences, made-up players and made-up colleges, so nothing in it is a real person. You create one quarterback and play his career.

Four archetypes

Pocket Passer (accuracy and timing), Scrambler (legs and improvisation), Game Manager (smart and safe) and Gunslinger (big arm, big risks). Each starts with different attributes and his own signature ability: Quick Release, Escape Artist, Film Room or Deep Ball.

College to the draft

You play one college game first. How you play it sets your draft stock: a sharp game in a win goes in the top five, a bad one slides you into the second round. Then draft night, and the team that picks you is yours for the season.

Play, moments or sim

Each week you can play the whole game, play only your drives (the moments) with the rest simulated, or sim it. Quarters default to two minutes; you can lengthen them in the settings. Simulated games earn less XP than games you play.

Getting better

Weekly goals and your play earn XP. Every 150 XP is a skill point; raising an attribute costs 1 point below 80, 2 below 88, 3 below 94 and 4 above that, so the last few points are the hardest. Six abilities unlock by level, from Film Room (read the defense right 85% of the time instead of 70%) to Ironman (your attributes decline three years later).

The season runs 18 weeks with the playoffs after it, then an offseason with contracts, aging and offers from other teams. Career saves in your browser only.

How to run a two-minute drill

The two-minute drill is the hurry-up offense at the end of a half: move the ball fast while saving the clock. The same principles win in the game.

1. Know what stops the clock

  • An incomplete pass stops it until the next snap.
  • A runner or receiver out of bounds stops it. In the NFL it stays stopped in the last two minutes of the first half and the last five of the game; in the game, inside the final two minutes.
  • The two-minute warning stops it once, at 2:00.
  • A timeout or a spike. A spike costs a down; a timeout doesn't.
  • A completed pass or run that stays in bounds keeps it running. In the NFL a first down does not stop the clock.

2. Use the sideline and the middle differently

Outs, comebacks and the Sideline signal get the ball out of bounds and stop the clock, for fewer yards. Digs and crossers over the middle gain more but keep the clock running, so pair them with a timeout, a spike or a no-huddle snap.

3. Save timeouts for the end

A timeout is worth most when the clock is running late in the drive, or to set up a field goal. Spiking early saves a timeout but burns a down you may need on third or fourth.

4. Play for field-goal range when a field goal wins or ties

Down by three or tied, you don't need the end zone. Get into range, run the clock down, and kick with as little time left for the other team as you can.

5. On defense, don't play soft too early

Prevent stops the deep ball but gives up easy yards underneath and lets them work the clock. Mix coverage with pressure, and keep your timeouts if you might get the ball back.

FAQ

Is The 4th Call free?

Yes. It's free in your browser on phones and computers, with no account and no download.

Does everyone play the same daily game?

Yes. The Daily is the same game for everyone, new at midnight UTC, so scores compare on the leaderboard. Your own plays are saved only in your browser.

Are the players real?

Teams and rosters are real NFL teams and players, with ratings computed from season statistics (nflverse). The game situations are made up.

What happens if the game is tied at the end?

It goes to overtime by the NFL's rules. You call the coin toss, and the winner chooses to receive or kick. Both teams get the ball at least once, unless the kicking team scores a safety on the first possession; after that, the next score wins. Daily, Survivor and Practice games use the playoff format, 15-minute periods until someone wins. In Career, regular-season overtime is one 10-minute period and can end in a tie.

How is this different from other two-minute drill games?

Three things. You call the defense as well as the offense, from 19 coverages and blitzes. Every call is graded against every other option you had, by simulation, so you learn why a call was right rather than only whether it worked. And the Daily rotates through goal-line stands, fourth-down decisions and protecting a lead, not only two-minute drills, with Practice to build any spot and a Career mode on top. No account is needed for any of it.

Is there a career mode?

Yes, in beta. Create a quarterback, play one college game, get drafted and play a full season in a fictional league, with XP, skill points and abilities between games. Every team, player and college in it is made up.

Is it affiliated with the NFL?

No. The 4th Call is an independent fan game, not affiliated with or endorsed by the NFL, its teams or the NFLPA.
